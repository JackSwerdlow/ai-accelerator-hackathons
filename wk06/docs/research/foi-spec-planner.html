1 Orchestration approach

How the four agents get wired together and run.

Why it matters. This is the spine — it decides how testable, debuggable and demoable the pipeline is, and how cleanly a team can split work. The brief only requires a supervisor that enforces a HITL gate.

2 Inter-agent contracts & team split

How parallel builders avoid colliding.

Why it matters. A small team building in parallel with AI. The highest-leverage move is to freeze the data contracts first, then let each person/agent own one stage behind a stable typed interface.

3 Structured-output mechanism

How we guarantee parseable agent output.

Why it matters. Flaky JSON is the most common reliability failure (the brief calls out "unparseable response"). Opus 4.8 / Sonnet 4.6 / Haiku 4.5 all support native structured outputs.

4 Core stack & CLI ergonomics

Libraries that shape the build and the demo.

Why it matters. The brief wants a CLI that processes a folder and prints a cost summary. Small choices set team velocity and demo polish.

6 Triage taxonomy

What the triage agent classifies into.

⭐ High human-input decision. Your knowledge of real FOI request types is the differentiator here. Edit the proposed taxonomy directly.
Why it matters. MVS needs one field, but "Excellent" wants accurate, useful classification that drives routing and complexity. Enums give reliable structured output.

7 Compliance / exemption decision policy

How RAG output becomes a recommendation.

⭐ High human-input decision. The biggest domain-judgment opportunity — absolute vs qualified exemptions, the public-interest test, when to recommend partial release.
Why it matters. The policy distinguishes absolute (s40, s41) from qualified (s36, s43, need a public-interest test) and supports partial release/redaction. A naive "ask the LLM" misses this structure.

8 RAG: embeddings, chunking & retrieval

How the policy knowledge base is built and queried.

Why it matters. The compliance agent must retrieve excerpts and cite them. The exemptions guide is already structured by section (s12, s21, s36, s40, s41, s43) — a gift for chunking.

9 HITL gate placement & "modify" semantics

Where the human sits, and what they can do.

⭐ High human-input decision. The brief mandates an approve/reject/modify gate that pauses, shows evidence, and logs a timestamped decision.
Why it matters. The design choices are how many gates, and what "modify" actually does to the outgoing response.

10 Evidence display & audit trail

How the system survives failures without crashing.

Why it matters. The brief requires: on agent failure (API error, unparseable, empty) log it and continue with a fallback. Lower-priority goal #4 adds model fallback. The Reliability axis rewards covering ALL paths.

12 Redaction approach

How we mask personal info before review (lower-priority goal).

⭐ High human-input decision. This is the s40 personal-data exemption in action — what counts, and how confidently to mask it.
Why it matters. A redaction agent masks names, addresses, emails in drafts before human review. Deterministic vs LLM matters for trust on a compliance-critical task.

13 Cost-tracking granularity & price source

How precisely spend is tracked and totalled.

Why it matters. The Cost-awareness "Excellent" band wants per-agent AND per-request breakdown — model, prompt tokens, completion tokens, estimated cost per call — plus an end-of-run summary.

14 Persistence: result & audit format

What artefacts a run leaves behind.

Why it matters. Required: a JSON result per request (classification, exemptions, draft, decision, cost). Lower-priority goal adds an audit log of every decision/override/cost for compliance reporting.

15 Batch processing & progress UX

How a folder is processed, given an interactive human gate.

Why it matters. Lower-priority goal: batch processing with per-request status, cumulative cost, and ETA. The tension: the HITL gate is interactive, so true parallelism fights the human pause.
